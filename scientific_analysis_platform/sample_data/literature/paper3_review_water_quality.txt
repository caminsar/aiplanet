Title: Water Quality Trends in the Middle Yangtze River: A Review of Nutrient Pollution

Review Snippet:
Nutrient pollution, particularly from nitrogen (N) and phosphorus (P), remains a significant concern for the water quality of the Middle Yangtze River and its connected lake systems like Dongting and Poyang. Sources are diverse, including agricultural runoff (fertilizer use), untreated or undertreated municipal wastewater, and industrial discharges. Studies analyzing monitoring data over the last two decades often report increasing trends in Total Nitrogen (TN) and Total Phosphorus (TP) concentrations, especially in tributaries and lake inflow areas. While large projects like the Three Gorges Dam have altered flow and sediment, their direct impact on nutrient concentrations is complex and debated, with some studies suggesting changes in self-purification capacity. Eutrophication risks are heightened in slower-flowing or impounded sections. Effective control of non-point source pollution from agriculture is a major challenge. Integrated water resource management and stricter effluent standards are critical. Keywords: Water quality, Nutrient pollution, Nitrogen, Phosphorus, Yangtze River, Dongting Lake, Eutrophication, Agricultural runoff.
