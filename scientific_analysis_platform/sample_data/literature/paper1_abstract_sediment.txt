Title: Impact of Dam Construction on Sediment Load in the Upper Yangtze River

Abstract:
The construction of large dams on the Upper Yangtze River, particularly the Three Gorges Dam (TGD), has significantly altered the hydrological regime and sediment transport dynamics. This study analyzes sediment load data from key hydrological stations upstream and downstream of the TGD, covering periods before and after its full operation in 2009. Results indicate a drastic reduction in sediment load downstream of the dam, with trapping efficiencies estimated to be over 70-80% in the initial years. This reduction has profound implications for downstream channel morphology, bank stability, and delta evolution at the river mouth. Long-term monitoring is crucial to understand the evolving impacts and to devise sustainable river management strategies. The study also briefly touches upon the potential increase in scour downstream due to "hungry water" effects. Keywords: Sediment load, Dam impact, Three Gorges Dam, Yangtze River, River morphology, Trapping efficiency.
