Title: Wetland Area Changes in Dongting Lake: A Remote Sensing Analysis

Summary:
Dongting Lake, a vital floodplain wetland system in the middle Yangtze River basin, has experienced significant changes in its area and ecological characteristics over the past few decades. This research utilizes multi-temporal Landsat imagery (1985-2020) to quantify these changes. Our findings reveal a general trend of wetland shrinkage, particularly prominent in the western and southern parts of the lake, primarily attributed to land reclamation for agriculture and aquaculture, and altered sediment-water regimes due to upstream dam constructions, including the Three Gorges Dam. The study identified an average annual shrinkage rate of approximately 0.5% over the study period, though with inter-annual variability linked to flood pulses and drought events. Reduced sediment input post-TGD is also discussed as a factor potentially slowing down natural siltation in some areas but also affecting substrate composition. These changes pose threats to biodiversity, flood retention capacity, and water purification functions of the wetland. Conservation efforts and integrated basin management are urgently needed. Keywords: Dongting Lake, Wetland shrinkage, Remote Sensing, Landsat, Land reclamation, Three Gorges Dam, Yangtze River.
